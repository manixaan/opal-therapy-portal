COMPLAINTS MANAGEMENT POLICY

1. PURPOSE

A complaint is information we would not otherwise have. This policy makes it easy to give us that information, and it commits us to responding to it without the person paying a price for having spoken up.

2. WHO CAN COMPLAIN, AND ABOUT WHAT

Anyone. A participant, a family member, a carer, a nominee, an advocate, a worker, another provider, or a member of the public. About anything — the quality of a support, the behaviour of a worker, a decision we made, a bill, an appointment, the way we handled an earlier complaint.

A person may complain anonymously. An anonymous complaint is recorded and acted on; we simply cannot report an outcome back.

3. HOW A COMPLAINT REACHES US

• In person or by phone to any worker.
• To {{OPAL_ORG_COMPLAINTS_CONTACT}}.
• By phone on {{OPAL_ORG_PHONE}} or by email to {{OPAL_ORG_EMAIL}}.
• Through a support person, advocate or interpreter — and we will help arrange one, at no cost, if that is what a person needs to be able to complain.

4. YOUR OBLIGATIONS AS A WORKER

4.1 Take it. If someone raises a concern with you, that is a complaint, whether or not they use the word. Do not tell them to put it in writing first and do not require them to use a form.

4.2 Listen before you explain. Let the person finish. Do not defend Opal, your colleague or yourself in the moment.

4.3 Never discourage a complaint, and never suggest that making one could affect the supports a participant receives. That suggestion is itself a serious breach of the NDIS Code of Conduct.

4.4 Record and pass it on the same day. Log it in the portal and tell {{OPAL_ORG_COMPLAINTS_CONTACT}}, even if you have already resolved it on the spot — a resolved complaint is still a data point.

4.5 If the complaint is about you, hand it over. Pass it to your supervisor or to [TO CONFIRM: the alternative complaints contact where the complaint concerns the usual contact]. Do not investigate a complaint about your own conduct.

4.6 Tell the person what happens next, in the words of section 5 below.

5. WHAT WE COMMIT TO

• Acknowledge every complaint within [TO CONFIRM: acknowledgement timeframe, commonly 2 business days].
• Tell the person who is handling it and how to contact them.
• Keep them informed while it is open, at least every [TO CONFIRM: update interval].
• Aim to resolve it within [TO CONFIRM: resolution target, commonly 21 or 28 days], and explain the delay if we cannot.
• Give the outcome, the reasons, and what we are changing as a result.
• Explain how to escalate if they are not satisfied, including to the bodies in section 7.
• Deal with the complaint fairly, confidentially, and without cost to the complainant.

6. NO DETRIMENT

No participant will receive a lesser service because they complained. No worker will be disadvantaged for making a complaint or for supporting someone else's. Any suggestion of retaliation is treated as a serious matter in its own right.

7. GOING OUTSIDE OPAL

A person does not have to complain to us first, and can go to any of these at any time:

• NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission — 1800 035 544, ndiscommission.gov.au
• AHPRA, for a concern about a registered practitioner — 1300 419 495, ahpra.gov.au
• Health and Disability Services Complaints Office (WA) — 1800 813 583, hadsco.wa.gov.au
• Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, for a privacy concern — 1300 363 992, oaic.gov.au
• Fair Work Ombudsman, for an employment concern — 13 13 94, fairwork.gov.au
• Police, for anything that may be a crime — 000 in an emergency, 131 444 otherwise

These contact details must be given to any participant who asks how to complain, and must not be withheld or delayed.

8. WHAT WE DO WITH THE PATTERN

Complaints are reviewed together, at least [TO CONFIRM: review frequency for complaint trends], by [TO CONFIRM: role responsible]. One complaint is an event; three of the same complaint is a system problem, and it is the second reading that changes how we work.

9. RECORDS

Complaint records are kept for at least seven years, separately from the participant's clinical record, so that a complaint never sits in a file in a way that could colour someone's clinical care.
